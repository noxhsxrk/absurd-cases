### 6. Acting & staging

- **Bodies:** box heads, box torsos, stick-box limbs. Faces are flat drawn decals (eyes, brows, mouth, stubble, hair scribble) that swap between drawings: half-lidded (default), wide, one-brow, squint, wink, closed-smile; mouth = a short line, a small "o", or a tall black hole. Brows exist only when an expression needs them.
- **The man:** default pose arms hanging, slightly slouched; power pose (hands on hips) for the accusation beats (shots 2, 4); never talks; expressions carry the story: bored (1) → alert (3, brows pop at 93) → suspicious squint (115) → smug blink (155) → deadpan aside (240–252) → jaw drop (585). Blinks are asynchronous (one eye slightly later).
- **Cops:** enter in step, stop, plant hands on hips within ≈10 frames (216–225) — the "we've got this" pose. Cop B holds the donut in nearly every shot he is in. Cop A is the operator: he moves the lamp, slams the table, works the fridge door, does the shouting.
- **Gesture timing:** hands-to-hips 4–5 frames; phone to ear 4 frames (168–172); donut bite up 3 / at mouth 3 / down 4; slam wind-up 3 up, 3 hold, 1 down; fist shake ≈16 frames; head turns 2 frames (whip) or 8 frames (unison turn).
- **Eye-lines:** man → fridge (80–125) → camera (240–252) → Nosferatu (581–595). In shots 14–15 everybody stares at the fridge/camera position; in 547–554 all three turn the same way, which motivates the whip pan.
- **Staging tricks:** three heads stacked vertically to fit a 9:16 frame (shot 14); three faces fanned across the fridge opening (shot 9); 3-shots always shoulder to shoulder on the red rug (the rug is the "stage mark" in front of the fridge).
- **Secondary motion / squash-stretch:** cap pops off cop A's head and settles (600–607); cop B's head box stretches ≈ +25 % taller with the cap floating (610–616); Nosferatu's cuffed fingers splay and sag. Ties and clothes do not move.
- **Idle life:** cat clock eyes and tail swing in every kitchen shot (53–84, 126–159, 418–457, 560–580, 627–682, 702–727); spider hangs in the window and on the wall; blinks on every held face; mist in the fridge; dust motes in the lamp cone; street lamps streak past the car window.
- **Who operates props:** man — fridge door (off-screen hand in shot 3), phone; cop A — fridge door, desk lamp; cop B — donut, cookie plate; Nosferatu — wall switch (3D sleeve + fingertip), car dome switch (3D claw).
- **How the non-human characters act:** the milk has **no face, no limbs** — it "acts" only by condensation and by being lit like a suspect. Nosferatu is a **photographic cut-out**: a full-figure image in the arch that never changes (frame diff 0.6–1.0 while he toggles), and a head-and-shoulders image in the car that has exactly two states — neutral (768–799) and grinning (800–830) — swapped on one frame under cover of the dome light coming back. There is no in-between animation; the rest of his acting is done by an attached low-poly arm/claw and by the light he controls. From behind and in the cuffs shot he is a full low-poly model.
- **Background/crowd:** none. No extras; the street has houses with lit windows only.

### 9. Scale of the escalation

This clip escalates by **absurd specificity, not by quantity** — there is no army, no weapon, no damage:
- Police response: exactly **2 officers, 1 car** ("their two best men"), arriving 1.33 s after the call tap.
- Interrogation of an object: 1 bottle, 1 lamp, 1 plate of chocolate-chip cookies, 1 table slam; 3 shots, 4.7 s (276–417).
- Evidence test: fridge door opened 3 times in 1.33 s.
- The repeated offence: 16 room-light changes (15 visible, 1 hidden by the cut at 627; 5 of them in one locked 1.87 s shot) + 3 dome-light changes in the car.
- Punishment: "on his way to prison" — no sentence length, no court.
- Physically impossible things played straight: milk treated as a suspect who can talk; a black-and-white photo figure standing in a kitchen operating a switch; a cut-out photo riding in a patrol car; his arm reaching the partition switch through the cage; police arriving while the phone is still ringing; a head stretching in surprise.

### 10. Deviations from the known rules

**Formula**
- CONFIRMS: "This man <ordinary thing>" opener; object does what it does (the fridge light goes off); "That was a <CHARGE>" with plate; "So he called the police." word for word; one institution gag (interrogation, good cop / bad cop).
- BREAKS: police do not over-react with force or numbers — the joke is two cops and a milk bottle. **No sentence and no 2–5 word verbal coda**; the last line is a plain "Now he's on his way to prison." and the punchline is a **silent 1.6 s visual coda**.
- NOT COVERED BY THE LIST: a **mystery/twist structure** — the accused object (milk) is a red herring; the real offender is revealed late by a whip pan, shown before he is named, and is a **famous fictional character** (not an object, animal or weather), rendered in a different medium (B/W photo in a 3D world). The reveal is foreshadowed (eyes in the arch, switch on the wall, Halloween props).

**Voices**
- CONFIRMS: the lead never speaks. The object (milk) has no face.
- One quoted character line; pitch 252.8 Hz vs 100–170 Hz narration — second voice or narrator shouting: uncertain.

**Shot lengths / ending**
- Median 0.87 s (a little faster than 1.0–1.2); action runs 0.27–0.50 s (faster than 0.4–0.6 at the low end); nothing over 1.87 s.
- BREAKS: **no frozen last frame** (6 moving dark frames after the final light-off; possible platform trim — uncertain). CONFIRMS: no outro.

**Camera**
- CONFIRMS: most shots push in; fast arrivals that settle in 3–8 frames; roll 5–15° on emotional CUs (upper end exceeds the 2–10° rule); motion blur on fast moves.
- NOT COVERED: stepped snap-zooms on consecutive words inside one take; impact snap-zoom instead of camera shake; one deliberately locked-off shot for deadpan repetition; object-POV bookends.

**Charge plate**
- CONFIRMS: navy slab, top of frame, arrives on the charge word (2 frames early), settles in 6–7 frames with a wobble.
- BREAKS / differs: left-aligned and bleeding off the left edge, not centred; no visible hanger; appears once only (no repeat, so no re-drop to observe).

**Captions**
- CONFIRMS: small white with dark outline, ≤ 2 lines, ≈ 78–81.5 % height (lower line slightly below the 76–80 % band), hard swap, quotes for character speech, `f***`.
- BREAKS: cards are whole sentences of 3–8 words (4 of 15 exceed 5 words); the coral keyword appears on only 3 of 15 cards (charge, "police", culprit's name). Bleep: cannot verify (Whisper transcribed the word, so it may be audible — uncertain). No em-dash line present.

**Phone / UI / annotations**
- BREAKS: the phone does **not** slide up from the bottom; it is a top-down in-hand POV insert (8 frames) followed by a phone-to-ear CU (8 frames). UI is minimal (3 words). CONFIRMS: pause-bait props (BUY MILK note, handle on the phone case, eyes in the arch). Red annotations: not present.

**Police FX**
- Strobe: lightbar alternates every ≈ 8 frames (slower than 3–5) and only tints the exterior shot; it never washes an interior. Muzzle flashes, casings, dust-brawl cloud: not present (no violence in this clip).

**New devices worth adding to the rule list:** light toggles as a rhythm instrument synced to words; silent visual beats between narration lines (5–7 of them); look-to-camera aside by the silent lead; cascade of shortening reaction shots; continuity prop carried through the episode and dropped on the reveal; reveal-before-name (17 frames).

### 11. Steal list

1. **Reveal before you name.** When a culprit or twist appears, show it for 15–20 frames with no caption and no narration, then let the narrator name it (here: 560 on screen, caption at 577).
2. **Shortening reaction cascade.** After a reveal, cut single CUs of each witness at 15 → 12 → 9 frames (one new expression each: jaw drop, hat pop, prop drop), then a 10-frame group hold. Budget ≈ 1.5 s of reaction per big reveal.
3. **Stepped snap-zoom on consecutive words.** For a 3–4 word beat, keep one take and jump the camera in 3 steps of 3 frames (≈ +35 % each, motion-blurred), each landing 1–3 frames after its word, instead of cutting.
4. **Use a light as a metronome.** If the story is about a light, toggle it in-scene on specific words; make every change 2 frames (one mid-luma frame), keep "off" at ≈ 65–70 % of "on" brightness so the frame still reads, and allow 3-frame "on" blips for comedy.
5. **Lights-out eyes.** When a room goes dark, darken skin/costume and swap eye decals for big white discs with small black pupils; move the pupils every 4–6 frames; hold 15 frames.
6. **Lock the camera for deadpan repetition.** When an offender calmly repeats the offence, use a completely static shot of ≈ 1.8 s with 4–5 repetitions at uneven intervals (5–15 frames) and move nothing else but one idle prop.
7. **Object-POV bookend.** Open from inside the object (door closing on one face, light fading over 5 frames); later reuse the exact camera with 4 black frames, then the door opening on several faces.
8. **Objects act through physics, not faces.** Give an accused object no face; put it under a practical lamp with a visible cone and let one condensation bead run down it over 12–18 frames on the line "it didn't talk".
9. **Stage idioms literally with prop puns, synced to the words.** "Good cop" = a 9-frame gift slide on the word "good"; "bad cop" = 3 up / 3 hold / 1 down slam hitting on the word "bad", with a 2-frame +30 % snap-zoom instead of shake.
10. **Silent lead, look to camera.** After a boastful narrator line, whip the lead's head to the lens in 2 frames and hold a one-brow deadpan for 12 frames while the boasted-about characters pose behind.
11. **Plant the answer and a carried prop.** Put the culprit's eyes in a dark doorway for 3 frames in the second shot and keep the tool (switch) in frame from the start; give a side character one prop (donut) to hold all episode and drop at the reveal, and leave it on the floor in the next wide.
12. **End on a silent visual coda.** After the wrap-up line, add a ≈ 1.6 s caption-less shot in which the offender repeats the offence in custody: effect first with the cause off-screen (light off 6–7 frames), swap his expression on the frame the light returns, then reveal the hand and do it once more; cut out within 6 frames of the last beat.
