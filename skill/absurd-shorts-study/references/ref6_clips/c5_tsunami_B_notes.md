### 10. Deviations from the known rules (part B)
**Formula**
- CONFIRMS the skeleton but runs it a second time inverted: ordinary act ("snuck onto a cruise ship") → the offender does what it does (spots him) → "So it called the police" → charge plate → sentence → coda. The caller is the weather, the accused is the lead.
- NEW: a sequel act inside one clip (66 s total), a time-skip card ("Months later,"), a three-flash recap, a prison-break mini-heist with accomplices, a TV-news insert.
- Coda "With its family." = 3 words: CONFIRMS 2–5 words.
- Institution gag: prison (break-out, then the lead inside): CONFIRMS one institution; the police response to the human is under-played, which BREAKS "police over-react literally" for this half.

**Voices**
- CONFIRMS: lead never speaks, narrator voices everything, offender is weather. No quoted captions in part B.
- NEW: narrator switches to a newsreader register for "Breaking news:" (pitch up ≈ 60 %, caption all coral + colon).

**Rhythm**
- Median 1.10 s: CONFIRMS 1.0–1.2 s. Action shots 0.47–0.63 s: CONFIRMS 0.4–0.6 s.
- Last frame frozen ≈ 15 frames: CONFIRMS 10–24. No outro graphic: CONFIRMS. NEW: a 3.57 s wordless tableau after the last word, inside one 100-frame shot — a silent visual epilogue rather than an outro.

**Camera**
- Push-ins 61 % of shots (rule says ≈ 79 %): lower, because this half has more tracking/crane/prop-follow shots. Fast-settle arrivals 17 % (rule ≈ 27 %). Roll up to 15–20° on two shots (rule 2–10°): BREAKS upward. Motion blur: CONFIRMS.

### 11. Steal list
1. **Role-reversal sequel:** when an episode runs long, replay the formula inverted — offender spots the lead committing a petty offence and "calls the police"; give the lead the offender's exact sentence, in the same numbered cell, with the damage from the escape visibly repaired.
2. **One-word-per-flash recap:** for "it remembered…", cut three 14-frame reprises of earlier shots, each opened by 2 white frames (≈ 90 % then 50 % white), graded grey-teal with vignette, each cut on a word; return by plain hard cut.
3. **Give a faceless offender eyes with a prop:** binoculars on a stalk that extrude in 5 blurred frames, look past the lead, then swing down and lock on (4–5 frames); push the camera into the lenses (×1.9 in 1 s) to hand over to memory.
4. **Deadpan reveal by wardrobe:** keep the lead's mouth fixed; let sunglasses slide down in 5 frames to expose the eyes, hold 7 frames, and keep them skewed for every later shot.
5. **News-HUD overlay for a genre switch:** pop a LIVE bug (top-left) and a two-tier lower third (red tab + dark banner, y 79–89 %) in ONE frame, 1 frame before the word "Breaking"; keep it across the next cut (≈ 4 s total); move the caption up to ≈ 74 % while it is on; drop it on a cut.
6. **Heist in three sub-second shots:** tool rises (12 f) → cut INTO the tool's point of view → effect ECU (17 f) → result (17 f, action already under way on frame 1) → hold 30+ frames on the empty aftermath with one bystander reaction.
7. **Do the whole charge beat in one take:** accuser's arm pokes the accused (hold 16 f), officers walk in over 10 frames, plate drops on the charge word (enter → lowest in 3–4 f, rebound, rest by ≈ 11 f), then hold 20 frames in silence before the next line.
8. **Sentence plate as set piece:** build the sentence board into the cell lintel and flip it down in 3–4 frames on the last word; push in ≈ +25 %/s during the hold.
9. **Cut on the first word of the clause or 6–11 frames before it;** never more than about 5 frames after (Whisper onsets, loose on a few words). Put the longest pause (≈ 1.3 s) on a wide moving shot and start the next location inside that pause.
10. **Tease the stowaway before the reveal:** eye dots in the air holes for ≈ 10 frames during the push shot; then door open in 4 frames (one blurred), 4-frame stare, step out, one prop-free gesture (shoulder flick, 6 f).
11. **Wordless epilogue:** after the 2–5-word coda, let one frontal tableau run ≈ 3.5 s with a single small action (kids hop in, 12 airborne frames, 8–10 droplets), remove the caption ≈ 18 frames after the last word, and freeze the last ≈ 15 frames.
12. **Brand on the set, not on the UI:** print the handle on one large foreground prop (life ring) and one background sign (parasol tag) in the closing location.
