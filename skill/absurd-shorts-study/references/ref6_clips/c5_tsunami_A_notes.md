### 11. Steal list (from part A)

1. **Cut ahead of the voice.** Between sentences, cut 5–9 frames before the first word. Inside a sentence, cut on the phrase boundary where the caption card swaps, on the last frame or two of the outgoing word.
2. **Sentence flip-board.** Put a blank navy board on the cell header from the shot's first frame; flip it in 3 frames on the word "years"; add a camera kick that settles in 3–4 frames; hold ≥ 40 frames; keep it in the set for later shots.
3. **Silent topper.** Reuse the same board for a second prisoner with a bigger number, on screen ≈ 11 frames, with no narration.
4. **Faceless offender, one arm.** Give an object/weather character no face and a single tube arm with a ball fist; let it creep (30 f), fist-bump (3 f contact), draw and tap.
5. **Element chemistry as the punchline.** When two elemental characters touch, show the physical result: 18 frames of growing steam spheres plus 5 droplets, no flash.
6. **Character white-out wipe.** To leave a scene on a pun, push into a white character for ≈ 12 frames until the frame is fully white, hold 2 frames, cut.
7. **Body wipe.** For "everyone was busy", block the lens with passing bodies for 3–9 frames, then reveal the group already moving away.
8. **Plant early, pay late.** Show the target (guard, sign, clock) in a 1.1 s reaction cut ≈ 16 s before the plan needs it; show the tool's test (scorch mark) before its real use.
9. **Crew roll call.** Three sentences of 4–6 words, one or two shots each, one prop each (magnifier, placard, dashed empty box).
10. **"Meanwhile" cutaway.** Change palette completely, hold the quiet wide for 35 frames before anything breaks, then escalate in two shots and pull out −17 %/s for scale.
11. **Whip-pan connector.** In a 0.8 s narration gap, use a 14-frame blurred pan between two characters to say "they are waiting".
12. **Charge plate geometry.** 89 % of frame width, top at 19 % of height, in at frame 0, overshoot at +3, settled at +6, hold ≥ 24 frames; have the lead look up at it.
