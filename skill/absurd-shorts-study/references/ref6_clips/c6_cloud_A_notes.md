### 10. Deviations from the known rules  (part A)

**Formula** — CONFIRMS for the first 16 s, compressed: "This man <ordinary thing>" → weather does what it does → charge → police → literal over-reaction → court → sentence. Differences: the charge is reported speech inside one sentence ("…informed the cloud that this was negligence and called the police") instead of "That was X. **Shot length** — BREAKS on the fast side: median 0.77 s, mean 0.90 s (formula section median 0.70 s) versus 1.0–1.2 s. Action runs 0.40–0.70 s: CONFIRMS. Frozen last frame / no outro: not in part A.

**Camera** — push-in is the biggest class, CONFIRMED in kind but lower in share here: 50–53 % of shots at a ≥ +5 % threshold (rule: 79 % — not confirmed for this clip; a looser threshold would raise it, since only 5 shots are truly static). ### 11. Steal list  (rules for a three.js/Remotion team)

1. **Sequel acts for a long special:** finish the normal formula by ≈16 s, then give each further act one sentence pair (set-up sentence + pay-off sentence, ≈3–5 s, 2–3 shots each) and let the sentence of act N be the set-up of act N+1 (prison → escape → good behaviour → early release → community service).
2. **Pay the opening complaint back as the punishment:** whatever was "missing" in beat 3 (here the wet-floor sign) must reappear, multiplied, as the sentence; end that beat on a 7-frame crane-up to a high wide showing ≥ 30 instanced copies, timed to the noun.
3. **Plant the offender in frame 0** in the top 20 % of the first shot and keep it directly above/behind the lead's head in the next two shots before the narrator names it.
4. **Give a faceless offender a point of view:** for every shot looking up at it, cut to a shot looking down from it with its edge in the top of frame (0.4–0.8 s each).
5. **Offender acting kit without a face:** a whistling music-note icon (10–15 frames) for "innocent", thin arms with mitten hands that hold every prop, and a hanging label that is physically swapped (flick off in 3 frames, new one drops in 4 frames + 3-frame bounce) whenever its status changes.
6. **Sentence as set dressing:** show the sentence on a sign that belongs to the location (cell label, gate board) in a different typeface from the charge plate, and amend it in-world with a 3-frame flip to red text when the sentence changes.
7. **Occlusion wipe:** when a character is thrown/removed, fly it through the lens so it fills the frame for 2 frames and reveal the new state (sign, new pose) as it clears — no cut needed.
8. **Plan-then-execute:** draw the scheme as a 5-icon chalk storyboard in a top-down insert (one icon per 5–8 frames, ≈1.1 s), then execute exactly those icons in the next two shots so the audience recognises it without narration.
9. **Idiom-literal POV:** for "saw nothing / didn't notice", cut to an 18-frame POV of a single flat colour with one prop (mug) rising into view, then cut back to the catastrophe behind the unbothered character and hold ≥ 30 frames with one small absurd life sign (goldfish) crossing.
10. **Document insert:** top-down page, hand letters appearing ≈1.5 frames per letter synchronised to the spoken words, then a stamp held down 9 frames and whipped away in 2 frames; hold the stamped result 10 frames.
11. **Impact without shake:** 1–3 smear frames + 6 radial white dashes (5 frames) or 3 dust puffs (4 frames) + a cut on the impact frame to a new angle; keep the camera steady.
12. **Caption/cut timing:** bring each card on at the first word's onset and clear it ≈5 frames after the last word; put cuts on word onsets or 2–9 frames before a sentence; use every 0.4–1.0 s narration pause to hold the previous gag's result rather than adding words. For the emergency beat, pulse a red full-frame tint on a 12-frame sine instead of a hard strobe.
