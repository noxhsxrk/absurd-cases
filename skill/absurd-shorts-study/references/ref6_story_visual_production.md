## 1. How the stories are told

**The formula is the first three seconds, not the episode.** "This man <ordinary thing>. That was <CHARGE>. So he called the police." is done
by 3–6 s in the short clips (c4: 3.0 s) and by ≈ 16 s in both specials. Everything after it is where the clip earns its laugh.

**Story shapes seen (one per clip; none is the plain "arrest → court → prison → coda" run):**
1. *Overkill with collateral* (c1): the responder is swapped (military), the over-reaction takes 9.6 s = 33 % of the clip, and it is a
   three-step build in three clipped sentences — "It hit the tornado. / It also hit the neighborhood. / All of it."
2. *Wrong suspect, then the real one* (c2): the obvious object (milk) is interrogated and "didn't talk"; the culprit is someone from
   outside the genre (Nosferatu, a black-and-white photo cut-out in the 3D world). He is planted early (eyes in the dark arch for 3 frames
   in shot 2, the light switch in frame from the start) and is shown 17 frames before the narrator names him.
3. *A charge with no offence; the police are the fools* (c3): "It hadn't done anything. That was suspicious." Each thing the cloud naturally
   does is renamed as a human act (thunder → "Shots fired!", rain → "it cried", clearing → "then it was white" → "an innocent cloud").
   Nobody is sentenced; the institution gag (therapy) lands on an officer.
4. *A third party argues with the premise and becomes the offender* (c4): the fixed line is cut off ("So he called the po—") by a man who
   says "That's not assault. That's science." His lecture is fast-forwarded ×3; being annoying is the second charge (TORTURE); the police
   beat him up. No court, no prison, no sentence.
5. *Role reversal* (c5, second half): months later the offender spots the man doing something petty and "calls the police"; the man gets
   the offender's exact sentence in the same numbered cell, with the escape hole bricked up.
6. *Sequel acts* (both specials): after the formula each further act is one sentence pair (set-up + pay-off, 3–5 s, 2–3 shots), and the
   pay-off of act N is the set-up of act N + 1 (prison → escape → "good behavior" → early release → community service → new offence → flees
   the country → second charge → Interpol → FBI wanted page).
7. *"Meanwhile"* (c5): another episode's event is the distraction ("an earthquake hit. The police arrested the entire earth. Every officer
   was busy."). c6 does the same with c4 ("The police were busy beating the shit out of science. So the cloud just left.").
8. *Under-reaction* (c2, c5-B, c6-A): "They sent their two best men." A small response can be the joke; a human's arrest is deliberately tiny
   (2 officers, no lights, one 2.3 s take) next to the spectacle given to the weather.

**The fixed line is a toy:** interrupted mid-word (c4), aimed at a different responder (military, Coast Guard, Interpol), spoken by the
offender (c5), or said a second time for a second charge (c4, c6).

**Sentences:** 4–5 words on average in the short clips (c4: 49 words in 12 sentences). Triplets of very short sentences carry escalation.
Heist grammar for crews: "The sun had a magnifying glass. The cloud had cover. They just needed a distraction."

**Punishment is a job that fits what the offender is:** the tornado "powers a wind turbine" (an officer whips it so it doesn't slow down);
the cloud "had to water every garden". The opening complaint comes back as the sentence, multiplied: the missing wet-floor sign becomes
≈ 35 wet-floor signs down the street.

**Closers seen (six clips, six different kinds):**
- cold reminder of the forgotten victim — "Oh, and the dog is dead." (c1)
- a silent picture gag after a plain last line: in the patrol car he switches the dome light off again (c2)
- a dry consequence on an official — "He's been in therapy ever since." (c3)
- the literal result — "He wasn't smart anymore." then 1.7 s of pictures: the cop turns the same test on the caller (c4)
- symmetry — "The man got ten years. The tsunami spent them at the beach. With its family." (c5)
- the threat is still out there, back at the opening situation — "It was last seen following this man." (c6)

**Silence is written in:** 5–7 caption-free picture beats per clip sit in the narration gaps (0.4–1.3 s) and hold the result of the gag just
told. Two clips hold ≈ 24 silent frames on a reveal before the reaction line.

**Shared universe, concretely:** the same event is told from each accomplice's side in its own episode (the prison break appears in c5 and
c6); cell numbers (18, 19) and signs recur; one running prop does many jobs in an episode (the wet-floor sign is set dressing × 45, an ID
document, an icon, a banner and the final wipe).

**Quoted character lines** exist in the newer clips (captions in quotes: "Shots fired!", "Are you smart?" "Very smart."), 0–3 per clip.

## 2. How the pictures are built

**Weather and object offenders have no face — in all six clips.** They act through:
- props held on one tube arm or thin arms with mitten hands (phone, cocktail, placard, chalk, white flag, giant cuffs);
- a hanging label that states their legal status and is physically swapped when it changes (INMATE 19 → COMMUNITY SERVICE: flick off in
  3 frames, new one drops in 4 + a 3-frame bounce);
- state: scale, colour (dark = malice, white = innocent), a sprite (lightning), particles (rain), damage (12–15 bullet holes that stay);
- floating ♪ notes for "innocent whistling"; human mimes (pat pockets 15 f, shrug 13 f);
- their own point of view: for every shot looking up at the cloud there is a shot looking down from it (0.4–0.8 s);
- a prop for eyes when the story needs a look (binoculars on a stalk that swing down and lock on in 4–5 frames).
The human lead keeps a fixed mouth; expression comes from brows, sunglasses sliding down (5 f), a 2-frame head whip to the lens.

**Density lives inside the shot, not in the cutting.** c1 stacks 3–4 explosions in a 45–52-frame shot, 11–12 frames apart; old smoke stays
so the damage accumulates. Every physical hit lands on a stressed word within ± 1 frame, with a 1–2-frame whole-frame kick and no other shake.

**Moves that replace cuts** (c3 has 14 shots but 24 pictures):
- whip reframe inside a take: 4–8 blurred frames, a new picture every ≈ 0.8–1.0 s;
- stepped snap-zoom on consecutive words: 3 steps of 3 frames, ≈ + 35 % each ("light / went / off");
- tilt along the vertical axis of 9:16 (ground → person → sky in one move);
- occlusion wipe: a thrown character or a passing body fills the lens for 1–3 frames and the new state is there when it clears;
- white-out: push into a cloud / a white character until the frame is white for 2–4 frames, come out on the new camera;
- push through a screen: a picture inside the fake web page is a live render of the next scene; push ≈ 6 frames, hide the hand-over
  behind a full-frame object for 1 frame.

**Signs are props.** The charge plate is navy, ≈ 90 % of the frame width, top edge at 19–29 % of the height, no visible rope; it drops on
the charge word (± 2 frames), settles in 4–8 frames and is removed 1 frame before the cut. In c4 it is part of the scene: a character strikes
ASSAULT through with a marker (two strokes of 3 frames), SCIENCE swings in and knocks it away, TORTURE stacks under it, a bird sits on it,
and a baton cracks it so the letters fall past the lens. The sentence is never an overlay: it is a flip-board on the cell lintel
("10 YEARS", 3-frame flip on the word "years", small camera kick, held ≥ 40 frames), amended in-world (flips to red "5 DAYS"), and topped
silently with a bigger number for the next inmate (≈ 11 frames, no narration).

**Captions follow the voice, not the picture:** 1–7 words per card (whole short sentences are fine), swap on the first word of each phrase,
may run across a cut, clear 5–20 frames after the last word, and are absent in every pause. The coral keyword is optional: about 40 % of
cards have none, some colour a 2–3-word phrase, a few are coral in full ("Breaking news:"). Position 78–81 % of the height, raised to ≈ 74 %
while a news lower third is on screen.

**Light and colour tell the story:** the fridge light toggles ≈ 28 times as a metronome, each change in 2 frames, on specific words ("off"
keeps 65–70 % of the "on" brightness so the frame still reads; in the dark, eyes become white discs with small pupils that move every 4–6
frames). The sky is regraded inside a shot over 25–30 frames when the mood turns. An emergency is a red full-frame tint on a 12-frame sine,
not a hard strobe. A complete palette change marks a "Meanwhile" cutaway. Police red/blue washing the whole frame is rare here (one brawl);
usually it is only the light bar or a local tint on the road.

**Screens and documents are staged scenes:** the FBI "Most Wanted" page runs 7.6 s with only 3 jump-scrolls — charge cards slam in on their
spoken word, each with a tilted "exhibit" photo that is a re-staged render of the earlier beat; the cursor is an actor (underlines, ticks,
circles in red over 6–12 frames, gets electrocuted and stays charred). A news HUD (LIVE bug + lower third) pops on in one frame, 1 frame
before "Breaking", and survives a cut (≈ 4 s). A top-down logbook insert writes its letters at ≈ 1.5 frames per letter in sync with the
words, then a stamp is held 9 frames. Flashbacks: 2 white frames, then 12 frames graded grey-teal with a vignette, one flash per word.

**Plant and pay off:** the offender is in frame 0 (top 20 % of the first shot) before it is named; the tool of a plan is tested before it
is used; a side character carries one prop all episode and drops it on the reveal (the donut stays on the floor in the next wide); the
survivor of the story sits small in the background of the last set and is never mentioned.

**Branding** is on 3–5 objects in the world per clip (phone back, mailbox, number plate, life ring), readable only on pause.

## 3. How the clips are produced (timings in frames at 30 fps)

**Editing**
- Cuts lead the voice: between sentences the cut is 5–9 frames before the first word; inside a sentence it falls where the caption card
  swaps. The picture leads its caption by ≈ 4 frames.
- Reveal before naming: 15–20 frames of the culprit on screen with no caption or voice, then the name.
- Reaction cascade after a reveal: single close-ups of 15 → 12 → 9 frames, then a 10-frame group shot (≈ 1.5 s in all).
- After a run of 0.2–0.7 s shots, one locked-off shot of 1.6–1.8 s with no zoom at all, for deadpan repetition.
- Before / after match cut for collateral damage: the identical camera, cut on the first word of the topper line, held ≥ 45 frames,
  caption gone for the last 25–30.
- Scale bracket for an institution: wide with the human at ≈ 4 % of the frame height (17 f) → medium on the action (42 f) → back to the wide (14 f).

**Camera (corrects A7):** push-ins are 41–61 % of shots, not ≈ 79 %; about a third of shots have no zoom. Where a shot pushes, it is fast:
median + 12–25 %/s, up to + 40–85 %/s. Fast arrivals 17–43 % of shots, settling in 4–10 frames. Roll reaches 15–25° in action (35–40° on the
jet). Punch-ins are tied to events: + 30–35 % in 5–7 frames as a plate lands or a weapon enters.

**Recipes**
- Lightning strike: 16-frame still wide → 2 pre-flickers → bolt for 1 frame → cut on the hit → 1 white frame → 6 frames of dark-blue
  background with a white halo and arcs redrawn every frame → 3 decay frames → the changed character (spiked hair, soot, 2 smoke puffs)
  held 15–25 frames, deadpan. No camera shake. Build-up is shown on the victim for ≈ 24 frames (hair rising, smile flips to a frown).
- Explosion: white-yellow core with a soft glow disc ≈ 60 % of the frame width (2–4 f) → orange fireball (6–8 f) → dark smoke that stays.
- Gunfire burst: three shots of 8–10 frames, a different officer and angle each, 1–2-frame white sparkles, 3–6 casings, one tracer line;
  then a 4-frame wide that proves the head-count; then the number as residue — a heap of 40–50 casings by a shoe.
- Beating: ground-level wide (first hit 6 frames after the cut, dust ball up in ≈ 6) → 13–17-frame ECU of the key word on the plate
  cracking and shedding letters → high wide with stars, swing arcs and legs sticking out. The contact frame on a body is never shown.
- Strike wind-up: 8 crouch → 4 sweep back → 4 hold → 2 smear → contact with 6–8 white radial lines + dust for 3 → 15–20 recovery.
  Gavel: 6 up, 2 hold, 2 smear, hit, 30-frame hold.
- Vehicle montage in under 2 s: 15 / 18 / 25 frames — glowing detail ECU, track along the body with heavy roll, head-on pass over the lens
  with 3 full-smear frames.
- Lock-on UI: corner brackets + centre diamond ramp white → yellow → orange → red in ≈ 2 s, with two step-zooms; label pops on the last word.
- Multiplicity reveal: 7-frame crane-up to a high wide of ≥ 30 instanced copies, on the noun.
- Gravity delay: the carrier vanishes in a 2-frame poof, the carried prop hangs 13 frames, falls in 5.
- Tag ending: red circle (6 f) → arrow (11 f) → circle (5 f) drawn over a living wide of the opening situation, fade 6 f, hold ≈ 7, cut.

**Endings (corrects "last frame frozen 10–24 frames"):** only one clip of six freezes. The others end live — a slow push with idle motion
(grass, one falling leaf) 12–16 frames after the last word, a punch-in, or a silent picture gag of 1.6–3.6 s with the caption gone.

## 4. What this changes for our series
- Write from a menu of story shapes and closers; do not repeat the previous two episodes' kind of ending (EP10–13 all ended "released, new job").
- Spend about a third of the clip on the over-reaction; go past realism (Nox, EP14) — or make the response comically small.
- Default: no face on a weather / object offender; give it the acting kit above (EP14's Earth has cartoon eyes; the reference would not).
- Plan 3 or more silent picture beats; land every hit on a stressed word.
- Prefer a whip reframe or a step-zoom to a cut when the place does not change; cut 5–9 frames ahead of each sentence.
- Sentences as flip-boards in the set; charge plate ≈ 90 % wide and usable as a prop.
- Choose the ending on purpose: frozen frame, live push, or a silent picture gag.
